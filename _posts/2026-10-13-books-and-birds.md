## Birds

For those that know me, they know I adore my wife. Well, my wife adores birds. They have always been a fascination, something beautiful and interesting, but never something I was as interested in beyond pointing at a colourful bird and thinking _"neat"_.

It wasn't actually until my mum recommended we watch the YouTube documentary  _"LISTERS: A Glimpse Into Extreme Birdwatching"_ that I honestly thought I could get into birding. Not in the same crazy competitive form portrayed in the documentary, but I can download some apps and make the effort to go look at birds every now and then. And I must say, it's super fun. 

I'm definitely no expert, but I really enjoy going to a new place and ID'ing a bird I haven't seen before. Or seeing a bird I haven't seen before in a park I walk often. 

I got a camera just for birding... Which feels like it could be the start of a whole other hobby, but one that has a bigger barrier to entry in terms of dollars $$$. But so far I have been happy with the results of a second hand Nikon D5500 I got on marketplace.

## Neuroplasticity works

Stay with me here, but with hobbies a big part of why I struggled to stick with them is that I was not getting the instant feedback I wanted. When you play a video game you instantly get a point for doing the thing. But, when you read a page in a book or go for a walk you are in your own head and things are slower. I attribute this to two things, having a weaker willpower at that time, and not understanding that Neuroplasticity would take some time to kick in. So what changed... 

Well the obvious thing I have talked about before is the gym. I strengthened the willpower muscle by forcing myself to do things I didn't want to do. Like, going to the gym when it's dark, raining, the bed is comfortable etc etc. I think over time that has made my brain a bit more resilient. I've proven to myself that if I do a thing I am not good at, that over time I will improve.
